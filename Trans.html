If you have a product or service that has both a very good passion sell and a good fear sell, could you do both in the same pitch? Would that work against each other, or is that bad?

The question is: if you have a product or service that has both a passion sell and a fear sell, could you do both in the same presentation? The answer is absolutely yes, but if you really know your market, you won’t have to. You’ll know which one will work. I mean, you can certainly point out that there’s a passion side as well as a fear side, but you should really focus on what you know your customer is looking for. That’s really what I’m stressing you do here, because that’s what they’re going to tune into. The other side of the equation — they probably will not be that interested in. But they’re going to want to know — you need to know going in what that person is. You don’t want any surprises. That’s why it’s so hard to sell to somebody that you don’t really know.

The second one was: even though they know they need something, they don’t always buy it. That was my point about the will. Even though people know they need something, they don’t always buy it, because the fears and passions things are not happening with them.

How do you use your three points to sell something on eBay — or maybe they don’t apply to selling online?

Good question. How do you apply these three points to selling online? The answer is that you do. It’s just that it’s not an in-person meeting. You still got to know who you’re selling to. If you’re selling on eBay, you want to know who your market is. Who are your customers? Who buys antique mechanical banks? I used to collect those, believe it or not. And make sure that when you do your eBay setup, your eBay settings and stuff like that, you’re targeting those specific people. Make sure you list, for example, on eBay Deutschland in Germany, because a lot of these collectors are German and Austrian. That’s an example of how you would use these techniques.

And then when you’re selling — this is obviously a passion sell. On eBay, most of the stuff that you’re selling there doesn’t really have a fear component. I’ll be honest with you: it’s mostly passion. It’s antiques, it’s collectibles. I don’t really see a big fear factor there, except in auction listings. Those are two reasons why I do it. And also, too, I love working with the entrepreneur community. I really do. I love meeting new people who are doing this sort of stuff.

The second question is: would you modify it at all if you’re in B2B selling? That’s really what you’re asking — you’re selling mostly to corporations, to large businesses. Would the strategy be any different? The answer is: a little bit, but not very much. Corporations do not buy anything. People in corporations buy things, and the decision makers have fears and passions just like anybody else does. You got to figure out what they are.

What is the biggest fear a corporate executive has? Not at all — I disagree with that. He’s got a bigger fear than that. What is it? No — being fired. Being fired, maybe because he bought something that wasn’t appropriate. But that’s not the right answer. The right answer is: being fired. When you walk into that office as a consultant, you got the word “fear” stamped on your forehead. You’re some crazy guy off the street who’s been out of work for six to nine months, whatever, and you’re terrifying that poor person. When you’re selling to middlemen, the sale is almost always a greed passion sell, because they’re not buying these for their own use. They’re buying them for resale. Their biggest fear is that these things are going to sit around in inventory forever. So you got to alleviate that fear. You got to convince them that the sell-through rate is going to be very high. Four Words About the Future

You want to predict the future? I’ll close on this. I’m going to give you four words. Seriously, you want to write these down — if you want to know where the future is going, I got four words that can help you get there. So seriously, I can do it in four words. It’ll only take five minutes — well, maybe four minutes. I’ll try to do it in four; Dee’s got to go home. Okay.

These are four things that I think we can all be fairly certain about the next 10, 20, 30 years, at least in America or North America.

1. Digital

The future is digital. Digital is the word. Brick and mortar is going out the window. You’re not going to see mom-and-pop retail stores on any commercial strip in five or ten years. Right off the — right now you’re not seeing those around here. It’s all going online. That Digital Life trade show that I was talking about is all about companies that are helping people bring their businesses from the real world into the digital world, and it’s phenomenal. Last year they had 500 exhibitors. Think this is a small thing — the world is going digital. Get on that bus. Don’t do brick and mortar when everything’s going digital. That’s number one.

2. Global

Number two: the future is going global. The national and state boundaries mean absolutely nothing anymore. Three weeks ago I bought something on eBay from a seller in Russia. As a boy growing up in the 1960s, during the height of the Cold War, if I had ever bought anything from a — in Russia, do you know what would have happened? I would have had the FBI in my living room the next day. You know, that’s how bad things were. Things are different now. Nobody thinks anything of buying from somebody in Kazakhstan. I actually have a client that is importing things from Kazakhstan. Remember Borat? He’s importing things from Borat’s country, and he’s doing very, very well with that. They actually have some wonderful wine in Kazakhstan. It’s interesting enough — you don’t think of Central Asia as being a wine-growing region, but it is. There’s some wonderful wines from Central Asia. He’s specializing in that.

3. Virtual

So the future is global, the future is digital, the future is virtual. This is different than being digital. Virtual means you’re not going to see fixed hierarchy corporations anymore, where you climb corporate ladders. What you’re going to see is more like free-flowing project teams. Think of — can you think of a lava lamp? Can you visualize a lava lamp in your head? It’s the best — it’s the best metaphor that I could think of. Think of a lava lamp. Right. Remember the globs of the fluids? They kind of go into each other and then they morph and they go different directions. That’s what we’re going to be seeing in the world of employment going forward.

We’re not going to be seeing fixed corporate pyramid hierarchies. What we’re going to be seeing is project teams that get together and work on a project, then one group kind of morphs over here and does that, and another one goes over here and does that, and this guy goes over there and joins up with a different group. It’s going to be like a lava lamp. That’s what the world of work is going to look like. We’re all going to be independent contractors in 30 years. I really do believe that. There will be companies, but they’re only going to have five or ten employees or anything like that. We’re going to see a real flattening of corporate hierarchies.

4. 24/7

So: global, digital, virtual. And then last but not least, the world is 24/7. The idea that you will have work days and play days is going to go out the window. Every day will be a work day. Every day will be a play day. Every day will be a study day. I work every Sunday for at least a couple of hours. On Wednesday afternoons I play for a couple of hours. That’s the world that you’re going to be heading toward. There’s going to be no such thing as Monday through Friday and then weekends, Saturday, Sunday. It’s all going to be 24/7. But that, I think, makes it a lot more exciting and a lot more fun to be — I don’t think that these are necessarily bad things. I think the world’s going to be a little bit more scary, but I think it’s going to be a lot more fun too.

And by the way, fear and exhilaration — neuropsychologists, guys who deal with brain cells, brain waves, and stuff like that, tell us that fear and exhilaration generate the exact same responses in your brain. When you’re really excited about something, when you’re really terrified, it sets off the same lights in the brain. You ever see those brain scan studies that they do? Fear and exhilaration are just two sides of the very same coin. Welcome to the 21st century and the year 2013. Thanks again, guys. You’ve been a great crowd.
